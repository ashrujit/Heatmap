# ETH/ON Research - 2026-05-20

## Objective

Design question:

Can ETH/ON book memory add a useful pre-open layer beneath profile context, then
survive RTH audit well enough to help read IB edge failures earlier?

The target is not a signal. The target is a context map that says:

- what zones had overnight supply/demand memory;
- whether that memory was fresh, old, rebuilt, contested, consumed, or flipped;
- how RTH treated those zones when IB tried to break higher/lower.

For this session the practical question was:

Did ON build and preserve meaningful interest below `050`, making the `060`,
`030`, and `008` probes failed lower auctions, or did RTH mostly sweep lower
inventory before the actual higher move forced chase?

## Context From Trader Read

Profile thesis:

- ETH profile overlapped with prior-day profile above prior-day VPOC.
- ETH was a double distribution.
- If a breakout higher failed and the hole between the two distributions
  accepted, a visit back toward prior-day VPOC / ON lows was possible.
- If the breakdown of the upper distribution failed lower and IB accepted
  instead, a break higher was possible.
- Upside target for a real break higher was the PD-1 high area around `360`.

Live difficulty:

- Early IB made both higher and lower breaks look unsustained.
- Shorts looked weak, but it was hard to hold a position through the failed
  attempts.
- Lower attempts were around `060`, then later `030`; the lower-break failure
  became much clearer after the washout to `008`.
- Ideal long locations would have been `030` or `060`; actual successful longs
  were around `123` and `128`.

## Day-One Answer

There was built-up ETH interest below `050`, but it was mostly old by RTH. The
data does not support labeling the lower break as "fresh pre-open demand held
below `050`." It supports a subtler read:

- ON created demand-memory candidates at `29052`, `29028`, `29016`, and `29000`.
- The fresh pre-open map shifted attention higher, mainly `29084-29136`.
- RTH tested lower zones and repeatedly failed to accept below them.
- `060` had the strongest combined ON+RTH book response.
- `030` was a prepared context level but not fresh local confirmation.
- `008` was a washout/reclaim, not a clean demand-memory print.

So the more honest hypothesis is:

RTH swept through older ON lower-memory candidates and the ETH profile hole, but
failed to build acceptance lower. Shorts could press, but could not keep lower
prices. Once price reclaimed and accepted back above, the auction forced chase
into the upper targets.

## Trader Follow-Up Interpretation

The day-one read should emphasize the special role of `060`.

`060` was not just an old ON context marker. It had resting pre-open interest
and then kept updating through RTH even as the auction repeatedly threw buyers
out of the position. That repeated survival/rebuild behavior is more important
than a static zone label. It suggests the useful live annotation is not merely
`old demand memory`, but something like:

```text
060 demand memory -> tested repeatedly -> held/rebuilt
```

The final poke below `060` after 10:07 did not create a clean new demand zone or
a clean new supply zone. That matches the live read: a short-lived poke-only
auction that reversed quickly. The absence of a fresh local row there is
informative. It says the auction was not establishing lower business; it was
forcing out inventory and then snapping back.

The 10:15 expansion candle then confirmed that enough business had accumulated
around the failed lower auction. This reframes the missed `045` / `080`
interaction: the hesitation was not because the data was unclear, but because
the earlier `060` stop-out made the same long thesis feel suspect while the
broader auction was still validating it.

The `123/128` entry should also be labeled more carefully. It was not a fresh
demand-zone entry. It was the point where price stopped auctioning lower after
sellers pushed hardest. The local book looked supply-leaning because sellers
were indeed pressing; the actionable read was that buyers absorbed that supply
after the lower auction had failed. Better shorthand:

```text
123 supply push absorbed after failed lower auction
```

This is exactly why the ON map and RTH edge audit need different vocabularies.
The ON map can prepare the zones. The RTH audit must describe the interaction:
tested, held, rebuilt, swept, absorbed, accepted, or consumed.

## Execution Ownership: LevelLedger vs ON Map

The `123` entry and later `188` add were LevelLedger trades, not hypothetical
new-ON-indicator trades.

The ON map's job would have been to answer the scenario-level question:

```text
Is the auction accepting into the ETH hole, or accepting at/above the upper
pre-market distribution?
```

For this day, the answer increasingly favored acceptance at/above the upper
distribution. The key confirming feature was not just price reclaiming. It was
that new book zones formed above `060`, not below it. Repeated pokes under `060`
did not sustain price lower and did not create meaningful new supply/demand
zones below. If the market were truly setting up for a selloff, the reverse
should have appeared:

- no acceptance above the pre-market HVNs around `107` and `130`;
- new dominance rows forming below `060`;
- supply rebuilding below failed supports instead of demand/supply interaction
  migrating back above the upper distribution.

That did not happen. The lack of fresh dominance below `060` is therefore not
an absence of information. It is part of the information: the lower break was
not establishing business. Once that was clear, the correct task became "find a
long build location," and LevelLedger already supplied the execution evidence.

## Design Implications

The future ON map needs age/state labels immediately, otherwise it will overstate
old context:

```text
29052 old demand memory
29028 old demand memory
29016 old demand memory / bracket
29008 contested or weak supply
29084 fresh pre-open demand
29096 fresh pre-open demand
29108 fresh pre-open supply
29120 fresh pre-open demand
29136 fresh pre-open demand
```

Useful labels for the first prototype:

- `fresh`: event activity inside the last 90 minutes before RTH.
- `old`: meaningful ETH zone, but no recent pre-open refresh.
- `rebuilt`: repeated same-side response after prior tests or stop-outs.
- `tested`: RTH traded into the zone.
- `held`: traded through/into zone, then reclaimed without acceptance.
- `consumed`: traded through and accepted away.
- `absorbed`: opposing side pushed into the area but failed to continue.
- `contested`: both demand and supply are meaningful, ratio weak.

The map should not render all old zones with equal intensity. Old lower-memory
zones are useful as prepared context, but the trader still needs RTH acceptance
or rejection to make them actionable.
