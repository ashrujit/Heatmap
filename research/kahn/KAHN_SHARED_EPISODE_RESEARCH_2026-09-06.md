# Kahn Shared Repair-Episode Research - 2026-09-06

Research findings and proposed contract, not approved execution policy.
Only research scripts and documents changed. No production edits, runtime
controls, campaign dispatch, build, or deployment in this pass.

Follow-up: the [frozen September 1-2 NQ stress test](KAHN_NQ_EPISODE_STRESS_TEST_2026-09-07.md)
strengthens the defended-group case but exposes unlimited resolved-repair waiting
and early weighted-BE retirement as material qualifications. The September 6
results below are preserved, not retroactively retuned.

## Conclusion

The shared auction mechanism is a useful model of these fixtures. NQ's granular
events can be treated as members of an attacked structure instead of independent
invitations to add. ES can use the same rules, including multiple event identities
covering essentially one defended area. This does not yet establish a complete
grouping rule or a profitable scale strategy.

The important NQ result is **two or three early adds without abandoning opposing
repair failure**. Repeated defense at the same area also appears: requiring a new,
strictly advancing individual rail for every add would miss that information.
An initially failed group can rebuild while the same repair remains active; that
is different from pretending its failed members survived.

There are still two substantive decisions: what makes a repair episode local and
complete, and which inventory a later group's failure should endanger. ES's delayed
second add and NQ's later risk exit make those questions concrete.

## The Shared Observer

The same direction-normalized rules run on ES and NQ. No GEX input, no new
geographic add gates, no minimum band count, and no time-bucket batching.

1. Track fresh same-side ownership and previously defended areas. Open an
   episode when an area is tested or relevant opposing ownership/hold appears.
2. Keep member histories: owned, tested, defended, failed, and newly rebuilt.
   An old untested root is not fresh continuation proof merely because it lives.
3. Track relevant opposing members by exact epoch-qualified identity. In the
   baseline, all tracked opposing members must formally fail; one neighboring
   failure cannot stand in for another live claim.
4. Require current favorable price clearance and surviving confirmation. Prefer
   actually defended members; otherwise require fresh owned evidence. Proof may
   precede formal repair failure and may lie behind the cleared repair area.
5. Emit at most one usable permission per episode. Additional holds or another
   completion signature cannot multiply that permission. A later distinct attack
   can renew the same area after the preceding episode ends.
6. Observe all later episodes after the add budget is spent. Observation, add
   admission, and inventory management are separate experimental layers.

An episode ends on renewed excursion beyond its pre-attack favorable quote
extreme with qualifying support and no live tracked opposition, on structural
failure, or on an evidence gap. A resolved repair can wait for later fresh proof;
a new attack supersedes that unused resolution. These are explicit research
choices, not established production invariants.

Membership currently admits forward/intersecting evidence relative to the root
with the existing two-tick tolerance. This is deliberately transparent but broad:
it is not yet a validated method for separating nested or overlapping repairs.
Covered tick intervals retain holes rather than treating the entire outer hull
as owned. Decisions occur after the complete same-timestamp capture sample, so
LL's candidate-before-rail update order cannot create an intra-sample early add.

## What The NQ Sequences Show

### Partial failure does not imply whole-group failure

The Sep 4 episode from 10:03:24 to 10:07:57 contains four favorable member IDs.
Supplies #35 and #36 are attacked. #36 fails at 10:06:58, while #35 holds at
10:07:10. Opposing demands #34, #33, and #39 fail in sequence, the last at
10:07:57. The permission rests on defended #35, not a majority of surviving IDs.

This is a concrete reason to preserve the group and member histories rather
than collapse the sequence into the latest favorable rail or reject it because
one constituent failed.

### The 10:30:59 opportunity is a rebuild, not universal survival

| Time on Sep 4 | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| 10:28:40 | New attack on the supply structure begins. |
| By 10:29:36 | Attacked supplies #53 and #54 have both failed; demand #55 remains live. |
| 10:30:36 | New supply #57 owns 29591.25-29592.50. |
| 10:30:59 | Demand #55 fails; price is through its area and new supply remains valid. |

Resetting the entire episode when its original attacked members fail loses this
candidate. Retaining the active repair, while explicitly marking the old members
failed, allows its rebuilt completion. The three-add simulation is already full
by 10:26:06 and therefore does not execute this fourth opportunity. The older
ten-unit-cap result was capacity reached, not an exit at 10:31.

This is also why "repair failed" and "our original sponsor survived" must not be
synonyms. Newly established evidence needs its own identity and validation.

### The same area can legitimately participate again

On Sep 3, fresh demand #92 at 29457.50-29459.00 supports clearance at 11:15:38.
The same area is tested again at 11:20:58 and holds at 11:22:07. Demands are the
long campaign's favorable side; opposing supplies #97 and #98 then fail, completing
another permission at 11:23:14. This is a new attack/defense cycle, not a second
add from an unchanged HOLD. The prior strictly advancing individual-rail observer
waited for new demand #100 and added at 11:23:49 instead.

Observation continues through 11:30 even when size is complete:

| Case | All formal-failure completion times, not all executed adds |
| --- | --- |
| NQ long | 11:02:26, 11:08:45, 11:15:38, 11:23:14, 11:26:40, 11:29:12 |
| NQ short | 10:07:57, 10:15:47, 10:26:05, 10:30:59, 10:42:38, 10:48:38, 11:02:37, 11:18:49, 11:20:37 |

The short's 11:18:49 completion follows another breached-and-rebuilt episode:
six attacked supplies fail, but newly established #83 is tested and defended.
Conversely, a resolved repair without usable proof must be remembered briefly:
at 10:48:38 new supply #68 arrives after demand #66 failed at 10:48:31. Neither
discarding every breach nor discarding every failure immediately captures both
sequences correctly. Expiry and nested-attack boundaries remain to be specified.

## ES Is The Same Question, Not Yet The Same Timing

On Sep 3, ten favorable member IDs cover just two contiguous price areas in the
first episode. Five defended IDs cover **one area, 7705.50-7708.25**. That is not
five independent reasons to load size. The same observer produces one permission
after the opposing repair fails at 10:59:51.

On Sep 4, the first add is supported by repeated defense of supply #26 at
7743.50-7744.00. Live pre-fill demands #4 and #5 prevent interpreting those holds
as uncontested repair failure. They fail at 10:08:18 and 10:07:36 respectively.
A cold observer that ignores them misclassifies the earlier defense. This makes
live pre-entry continuity a contract question, not a harmless implementation detail.

The second episode, 10:13:29-10:42:44, is a useful unresolved fixture:

- Demand #33 owns 7730.75-7732.25 at 10:14:11. Overlapping #34 owns at 10:14:41.
- #34 fails at 10:20:41, but #33 remains live.
- #33 holds again at 10:30:05; fresh overlapping demand #40 owns at 10:30:10.
- #33 and #40 fail at 10:42:36 and 10:42:44. Only then does the baseline complete.

The long wait is therefore not solely a stale list entry: opposing evidence
actually renews. The chart shows substantial rotation around that area. A smaller
local repair may still be a legitimate basis for another tranche, but allowing
that requires a nested/local failure definition. Simply discarding #33 or timing
it out because an earlier short add is desired is unsupported by this evidence.

Event density also needs qualification:

| Window | All rail transitions | Favorable OWN | Favorable HOLD |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| NQ Sep 3, 10:55-11:30 | 90 | 17 | 12 |
| ES Sep 3, 10:55-11:30 | 99 | 21 | 16 |
| NQ Sep 4, 10:00-11:30 | 399 | 29 | 84 |
| ES Sep 4, 10:00-11:30 | 93 | 21 | 18 |

NQ is much denser on Sep 4, but not on Sep 3. Repeated IDs and overlapping coverage
matter more than a fixed "NQ has many, ES has few" rule. The proposed explanation
in terms of liquidity, range, and detector settings is plausible, not isolated
causally by this study. CL and GC portability has not been tested.

## Completion Signatures And Controls

The baseline retains formal opposing failure. Two explicitly more permissive
alternatives also run: no-claim TEST-to-HOLD alone, and that defense plus recovery
beyond the pre-attack favorable extreme. Neither silently overrides an observed
still-live opposing claim.

Adding the departure signature moves NQ's first three permissions to
11:02:26/11:04:51/11:08:45 on Sep 3 and 10:01:09/10:07:57/10:09:39 on Sep 4.
For ES it does not solve early construction on the short: its second add remains
10:42:44. Faster on selected trend fixtures is not sufficient validation.

In eleven earlier same-session control roots, the formal-failure baseline adds
zero. The departure/raw-defense alternatives add twice to the third Sep 3 NQ
root and once to the second ES root. The NQ path trims a failed added group at
10:33:54 and exits via weighted BE at 10:34:55; the ES root fails at 10:04:55.
Those are meaningful counterexamples to treating every defended test as an add,
but not statistical evidence that the stricter baseline is superior. Several
control roots fail quickly, and all controls come from the same two dates.

"Fast through repair" is not yet a validated completion signature. Descriptive
quote traversal measurements range from about one second to 76 seconds among
the NQ baseline completions; ES examples also have long waits between first
clearance and typed failure. The final-known claim hull is used only for this
diagnostic, not as an input feature backdated to the episode start. No speed
threshold has been fitted or promoted to permission.

## Construction And Management Must Be Separate

Three management experiments use the same permission stream and add budgets:

| Experiment | Added-tranche failure | Later evidence after capacity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Root plus weighted BE | No dedicated group trim | Observed, not a new campaign anchor |
| Tranche groups | Trim that add when all its qualifying support fails | Does not automatically replace older tranches' support |
| Latest group governs campaign | Entire position exits when latest qualifying group fails | Anchor continues updating even without another add |

The baseline main NQ tranches survive through 11:30 under the first two methods.
The third method exits the Sep 4 short at **11:12:46**, despite its last add being
at 10:26:06. It had adopted defended supply #75 at the 11:02:37 completion; that
area subsequently fails. This is not the old failed-pending-sponsor promotion bug
hidden in a new name: the later group was valid when adopted and then failed.

The result demonstrates the need to decide **what a newer group's failure governs**:
its own tranche, some accumulated profit protection, or the entire campaign.
It does not establish that the whole campaign should exit, or that retaining the
original wide root risk is better. Capacity must not accidentally decide this.
The older source's add-driven promotion and the new cap-independent experiments
are distinct; existing HoldRoot/TightenRisk paths are not being claimed absent.

Risk-driven reductions and weighted-BE exits are simulated. Paid target harvest
is **not**: no target was invented, and the 11:30 liquidation is an outcome mark,
not a profit-taking strategy. Full scale-in/scale-out economics remain unvalidated.

## Reproducibility And Limits

- Inputs are current linked C# LL-engine transitions replayed from local
  MarketRecorder NQU6/ESU6 captures, plus captured BBO. This is not exact live
  runtime reconstruction. Earlier notes distinguish actual paused/restarted
  runtime state and historical source behavior from these forced roots.
- Studied windows have no capture gaps above five seconds. Requested sampling is
  one second, observed cadence about 1.1 seconds; unseen intra-sample activity
  cannot be reconstructed. NQ/ES cluster settings remain different, 10/3 ticks;
  confirm/failure settings remain 8 ticks/10 seconds and 24 ticks/20 seconds.
- Warm adoption includes only observations between requested window start and
  root fill. Failed-root retries start cold. It cannot recover earlier unobserved
  claims and does not reuse pre-fill failures. Cold results are preserved separately.
- Fill proxies use the first captured BBO at least 250 ms after permission,
  within two seconds of that requested time, plus one adverse tick. Support,
  new/live opposition, clearance, and favorable price versus average are checked
  again. These are not broker fills, latency estimates, or fill guarantees.
- Position-average accounting remains unchanged on reductions. BE is an explicit
  captured-BBO proxy; fees, partial/rejected broker orders, queue position, real
  harvest, and production lifecycle workers are not modeled. Cashflow output is
  hypothetical unit-ticks, not dollar or fee-adjusted P&L.
- All **56 tests pass**, including the older studies. Splitting every non-root
  rail into equivalent fragments preserves permission times and covered areas on
  **16/16 seeded paths**; truncating the future preserves prior permissions on
  **16/16**. These are representation/causality checks, not validation against
  different detector settings or genuinely different lifecycle information.
- Source hashes for CampaignContracts, CampaignPolicy, and the LL engine are
  unchanged from this pass's baseline. Manifests retain input and script hashes.
