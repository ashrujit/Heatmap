## Shared Auction Hypothesis: User Clarification

The user corrected the proposed separation between NQ extension scaling and ES
same-area accumulation. The working research hypothesis is ONE auction mechanism
represented at different granularities, not two instrument-specific scale policies:

1. Fresh same-side evidence establishes an extension or area of interest. The
   relevant group may contain one band or several; band count is not its authority.
2. Opposing repair attacks that newly established structure. Members can be
   tested, defended, failed, or replaced without each transition creating a new
   campaign episode or invalidating the entire group.
3. The repair fails. Participation may then be supported by renewed interest
   at the previously attacked fresh evidence areas, or by decisive continuation
   through the repair area. These are two completion signatures to investigate
   within the same mechanism, not separate ES/NQ engines.

This is a research direction, not approval of grouping rules, price-only failure,
or execution changes. A touch or fast move alone is not established permission.
Keep the evidence that invalidates repair separate from the evidence confirming
continuation; current typed-failure replay is one baseline, not a completed
definition of group-level repair failure. LL band density's relationship to
liquidity, range, tick size, detector settings, and capture coverage remains to
be measured. CL/GC portability is a later hypothesis, not a validated result.

Group membership must be causal and tied to the same structural development and
challenge. Do not use arbitrary time buckets, a required minimum number of bands,
or majority voting over granular events. Distinguish defended members from old
untested members; renewed evidence at an earlier area is not resurrection of a
failed rail identity. Do not require a strictly advancing individual band for
every tranche if renewed defense of the same group is the phenomenon being tested.

The intended outcome is two or three justified early adds followed by active
management and scale-out, not continual adding through 11:30 or an add quota.
The ten-unit prototype's 10:30:59 result means it reached its size cap, not that
its sponsor failed or the position exited. Later add-driven sponsor promotion
causes the separate higher-capacity path to exit at 11:12:57. The next comparison

individual-rail replay did not model scale-out or implement the group hypothesis.

## Conclusion

Keep failed opposing repair as the baseline scaling hypothesis. The evidence
does not justify adding on every favorable consumption event or lean rail.
It does justify investigating whether Kahn represents the repair cycle correctly.

The current implementation is a serial event gate with one candidate and one
repair slot. It can forget useful evidence and require another favorable event
after confirmation already appeared. Its confirming-rail location requirement
is also stricter than price reclaim with surviving sponsorship. These are distinct from the
question of whether the market actually offered a worthwhile add.

A persistent repair setup is the leading design candidate. Its validity must be
structural and tied to identifiable evidence; persistence alone is not permission.
Do not introduce an early `SponsorStackAdd` exception or implicit price-only
repair failure before the stricter baseline has been evaluated causally.

### 1. Tracking can erase a repair without tracking a new candidate

`CampaignState.ApplyScaleTracking` clears repair state before calling
`TrackScaleCandidate`. The latter can reject the new range because it is not
strictly beyond the retained candidate. A repeated or less advanced favorable
rail can therefore clear the repair while leaving the candidate unchanged.

Source: [CampaignContracts.cs](../../KahnRuntime/CampaignContracts.cs), lines
706-712 and 759-784. The existing `NewScaleCandidateResetsPriorRepairFailure`
self-test explicitly expects a reset for a new candidate; it does not establish
that clearing state on a rejected/no-op candidate is intentional.

Observed in NQ campaign `nq-short-campaign-20260904-165805-1b5117`:

- At 14:12:08, demand 29491.75-29492.50 formally failed. Kahn recorded the failure.
- At 14:13:08, fresh supply 29494.50-29496.50 appeared with current price 29490.50.
- That supply range was behind the failed demand, so the continuation-range gate
  did not authorize an add. Kahn emitted a candidate-tracking decision instead.
- The resulting state lost the repair and its failure. The retained candidate
  stayed 29476.75-29479.25, still timestamped 13:45:33.

The campaign's recorded state contains 24 candidate-triggered repair clears;
21 left the candidate evidence ID unchanged. One cleared a failed repair.
These are state transitions, not 24 demonstrated missed trades.

Runtime evidence: `NQ/decisions.jsonl` lines 58524-58525 and 58555-58556 under
`C:\Users\j\Documents\KahnRuntime`.

### 2. Favorable confirmation can arrive before the formal failure

`PressPolicy` exits while adds are suppressed, before tracking favorable rails.
When repair failure arrives it records `scale_repair_failed` and exits again.
It requires a later same-side ownership/hold event to consider adding.

On September 4 at 13:08:27 and 13:08:36, NQ printed favorable supply ownership
while repair suppression was active. Demand failures followed at 13:08:37 and
13:08:49. No add followed before another demand claim replaced the repair.

At 13:12:01, consumed supply 29539.25-29541.50 appeared. Demand
29538.50-29540.75 failed at 13:12:06. The supply range overlapped the repair,
but its ownership event had already passed while suppression was active.
At 13:12:21, another demand claim replaced the failed repair.

This confirms an event-order dependency. It does not establish that retained
supply was sufficiently current, defended, or well-priced to justify an add.
That is the research question: can joint current evidence complete the setup
without requiring an additional future rail event?

Source: [CampaignPolicy.cs](../../KahnRuntime/CampaignPolicy.cs), lines 309-326.
Runtime evidence: NQ log lines 56601, 56619, 56624-56634, 56769, 56787-56796.
The LL engine also calls `UpdateCandidates` before `UpdateRails` within a sample;
any redesigned observer must handle that ordering explicitly.

### 3. Repair matching is simultaneously narrow and permissive

Only the latest qualifying opposite claim is retained. Another opposite
`RailOwned`/`RailHeld` overwrites it and clears its failure status. Qualification
uses arena/progression/proximity; it does not require a represented retracement,
an identified favorable stack under test, or measured adverse progress.

Failure matching uses range distance within the sponsor buffer, not claim ID.
At 13:08:37, failure of demand rail #42 marked the stored repair from rail #47
failed; #47's own failure arrived about 12 seconds later. Their ranges overlapped.
This might be legitimate zone-level invalidation, but the contract does not
define why one member's failure is enough for the other claim.

Continuation then compares the new same-side rail range with the failed repair
range. It does not establish reclaim from current price alone. The 14:13 example
illustrates this difference: price was below failed demand, but the supply rail
remained above it. Relaxing that condition requires a defined alternative proof.

Sources: `CampaignPolicy.cs` lines 404-433 and 887-916;
`CampaignContracts.cs` lines 787-825 and 1003-1015.

### 4. No-add geometry also suppresses observation

`NoAddZonePolicy` emits a 90-second suppression for evidence within four ticks
of its range. It matches the evidence range, not necessarily current price.
The resolver returns one decision, so this can displace both repair observation
and repair-failure tracking, as well as block an order.

At 13:02:50, NQ demand failure 29559.25-29567.25 resolved to `inside_no_add_zone`.
Price was 29555, below the 29567.50 lower boundary of the no-add box. The rail
was close to that box. This behavior is consistent with current source, but
shows why a scale veto and the preservation of auction evidence need separate
definitions.

Counts include repeat holds and neighboring failures; 12 does not mean 12

### 5. Evidence resets need an explicit scale-state contract

`ResetEvidenceEpoch` replaces the LL engine and restarts warmup without resetting
campaign scale state. Rail numbers restart inside the new engine. Old scale
candidate/repair state can therefore survive an engine that no longer observes
those rails.

NQ had evidence resets at 13:18:30, 14:03:04, and 15:06:08 on September 4.
The 14:13 state still retained the 13:45 candidate across the 14:03 reset.
Any persistent setup needs epoch-qualified identities and a rule for suspension,
revalidation, or invalidation after an evidence gap. This does not itself imply
flattening the root position.

Source: [KahnRuntime.cs](../../KahnRuntime/KahnRuntime.cs), lines 1253 and
1273-1282. NQ log lines 57016, 58283, and 58556.
